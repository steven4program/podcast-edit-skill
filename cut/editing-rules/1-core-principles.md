# Core principles

## Core philosophy of podcast editing

Podcasting is the art of conversation, not a speech. Preserving natural feel matters more than achieving perfect fluency.

## Delete the earlier, keep the later (apply judiciously)

Later iterations are usually more complete, so:
- delete the earlier one (slip / residual)
- keep the later one (complete sentence)

## Boundaries of fine cutting (never crossed!)

Fine cutting handles only these types: stutter, filler, self-correction, residual sentence, silence. **Never delete user content for any other reason**, including but not limited to:
- "the speaker label might be wrong" → not fine cut's job
- "this sentence reads awkwardly" → keep it unless it's a clear stutter/self-correction
- "this is redundant with the surrounding context" → that's the rough cut's job (content level); fine cut does not make content decisions

**Principle: fine cutting is technical cleanup (remove slips), not content editing (remove information).**

## Podcast-specific exceptions

1. **Natural corrections are kept**: if the correction process sounds natural, keep the whole thing.
2. **Thinking pauses kept**: deliberate thinking pauses are part of podcasting.
3. **Conversation response time**: in multi-host shows, don't remove response time.
4. **Filler in moderation**: don't over-delete 嗯/啊 — keep the conversational feel.

## User preference

When the user says "the later version flows better", delete the earlier and keep the later.
When the user says "the earlier version flows better", delete the later and keep the earlier.
When the user says "keep it natural", be lenient with everything.
